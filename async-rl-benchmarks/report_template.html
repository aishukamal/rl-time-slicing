1 · Executive summary

Across four experimental phases we measured GPU duty cycles of veRL RL training under every combination of execution mode (sync colocated, async disaggregated), data policy (on-policy, bounded staleness, unbounded staleness), and workload balance (generation-heavy vs training-heavy). All final experiments run out-of-the-box configurations — the idle we report comes from the workload's structure, not from tuning.

The law all measurements obey: the slower pipeline side pins its GPU at ~90–99%; the faster side idles in clean per-step blocks whose duration equals the gen−train time gap. Bottom line for time-slicing: the dominant open RL recipe family — DeepSeek-R1 / DAPO-style long chain-of-thought reasoning RL — leaves the trainer GPU idle 46–64% of the time in minute-plus periodic blocks under veRL's own async mode, regardless of staleness policy. On-policy modes additionally idle the rollout GPU in anti-phase. Only the divergent regime (unbounded staleness on a train-heavy workload) offers nothing to harvest.

2 · Background: why time-slicing for RL

RL post-training alternates two heterogeneous computations: autoregressive generation (latency-bound, one token at a time) and policy training (throughput-bound, parallel forward/backward). Unless the two are perfectly balanced, one side's GPUs wait. The prior PoC (rl-time-slicing, disaggregated sync RL with fast GPU context checkpoint/restore) demonstrated that a second job can be slotted into those waits: duty cycle 41%→71% with two jobs, <1s context swap, no step-throughput impact, 33% total execution reduction.

This project's question: in which realistic veRL configurations does that harvestable idle actually exist, how big is it, and does it survive the async/off-policy modes designed to eliminate it?

3 · Phase 1 — Async disagg needs artificial starvation on a simple dataset benchmark-longtail/

Setup: veRL fully_async_policy (async disaggregated), Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct on GSM8K, 1 trainer + 1 rollout H100. Goal: reproduce the "square wave" (minutes-long alternating busy/idle phases) that makes time-slicing worthwhile.

Finding: with default configs the pipeline stays balanced — no square wave. Single-turn GSM8K generation takes seconds per batch; the queue stays full and the trainer never waits. Producing multi-minute idle required deliberately fighting the framework:

KnobValuePurpose (all artificial)
rollout.gpu_memory_utilization0.25throttle sampler concurrency
require_batches32 (1024 samples/step)inflate trainer's collection wait
trigger_parameter_sync_step4batch weight syncs
staleness_threshold0flush queue at sync

Result: 16 training steps, 4 full sync cycles over ~2.5h — post-sync trainer idle gaps of 5.3 / 3.1 / 3.9 / 2.5 minutes, ~20s collection waits otherwise. Weight sync itself: ~1s — the idle gaps are queue-flush + regeneration time, not transfer time.

{{IMG:P1_SQUARE|Phase 1: square-wave timeline reconstructed from logs — yellow bars are 2.5–5.3 min idle gaps after weight syncs at steps 4, 8, 12, 16}} {{IMG:P1_DUTY|Phase 1: GPU trace (iteration 1, same recipe) — trainer humps with gaps, sampler generation blocks, 45% anti-correlation}}

Phase 1 conclusions

  • veRL's async mode is engineered to prevent idle — a concurrent pipeline with a buffering queue.
  • The trainer is inherently faster than the sampler (autoregressive generation is the bottleneck), so starvation is the natural direction — but with cheap single-turn generation it must be induced artificially.
  • Time-slicing applicability judged at this point: on-policy modes high, fully-async minimal. (Phase 4 later sharpened this: it depends on the workload's gen:train ratio, not just the mode.)

4 · Phase 2 — Multi-turn agentic RL: the long-tail is natural benchmark-deepresearch/

Hypothesis: multi-turn search-agent workloads (deep-research style) starve the trainer naturally — samples finish at wildly different turn counts and the batch waits for stragglers.

Setup: CMU verl-agent-deepresearch (arXiv:2510.06534) — sync colocated hybrid engine (the mode their fork hard-requires), Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, MHQA multi-hop QA, up to 6 search→read→generate turns per trajectory, local Wikipedia BM25 search (pyserini wikipedia-dpr-100w behind a Serper-format HTTP shim — no external APIs), 2×H100 TP=2, 32 trajectories/step. Config deltas vs the CMU recipe were hardware adaptation only — no throttling.

Result (run 4 — 16 GRPO steps, ~97 min, complete 100ms trace; profile reproduced across 3 runs):

{{IMG:P2_TIMELINE|Phase 2: full 97-min colocated trace — flat ~100% training plateaus alternating with spiky, underutilized multi-turn generation phases}} {{IMG:P2_PHASES|Phase 2: per-step phase structure — generation (orange) vs training/logprob/ref/validation (blue)}}

Phase 2 conclusions

  • The long-tail is intrinsic to the workload — 2.5-minute generation phases with zero tuning (Phase 1 needed three artificial knobs for the same shape).
  • Colocated mode converts the long-tail into low utilization, not reclaimable idle. With TP=2 both GPUs act in lockstep (they idle and burst at the same instants — the ~34% idle is search-I/O barriers, straggler-thinned decode batches, and engine transitions, fragmented into sub-minute slices on GPUs that are still allocated). No second job can use it.
  • Getting reclaimable idle requires disaggregation — which motivated Phases 3–4. (A full port of this workload into the PoC's sync-disagg trainer was built and shelved: sync RL's idle is already structural, so the interesting question moved to async modes.)

5 · Phase 3 — Staleness sweep, train-heavy workload async-multiturn/

Question: does a multi-turn tool-calling workload create harvestable idle in veRL's own fully-async disaggregated mode with out-of-the-box configs — and where does the idle go as the staleness budget grows?

Setup: veRL main fully_async_policy — which natively supports multi-turn tool calling (its rollouter is the agent-loop stack; the only glue was a ~60-line WikiSearchTool since verl removed its in-tree search tool). Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, 512 HotpotQA prompts (search-R1 format), hermes tool calls against the local Wikipedia server, 1 trainer + 1 rollout H100, sync_step=1, no throttling. Tool use was genuine: ~5 turns/trajectory (max 16), EM reward climbing 0.17→0.45. Only staleness_threshold varied: 0, 1, 4, 8, ∞.

This workload turned out train-heavy: generation ~30s/batch vs training ~70s/step — multi-hop QA answers are short, so the multi-turn structure adds latency but not enough tokens to dominate.

{{IMG:P3_IDLE|Phase 3: harvestable idle per GPU vs staleness — train-heavy workload}} {{IMG:P3_TIMELINES|Phase 3: the five utilization timelines — s=0 anti-phase square wave; rollout (orange) gaps shrink with staleness until they vanish at unbounded}}

Phase 3 conclusions

  • s=0: both GPUs idle in anti-phase (mutual ping-pong; queue pinned at 0) — ~50% of the 2-GPU capacity harvestable, alternating devices.
  • s≥1: one step of staleness lets ~30s generation hide inside the ~70s training window — the trainer saturates (91%) and all idle migrates to the rollout GPU in regular ~30s blocks (48% → 34% → 28% for s=1/4/8).
  • Step time is flat ~72s from s=1 to s=∞: throughput is 100% trainer-bound; staleness beyond 1 buys queue depth and data staleness, zero speed.
  • s=∞ is a divergent regime — the rollouter never pauses (96% util) and the queue grows without bound. Nothing to harvest, and not a configuration anyone should run.
  • Bonus finding: async-at-s=0 behaves exactly like sync disagg RL (the freshness constraint forces the alternating schedule) — you don't need a special sync harness to study sync-RL idle.

6 · Phase 4 — Staleness sweep, gen-heavy workload async-longcot/

Question: the most-replicated open RL recipe family is long chain-of-thought reasoning RLVR — DeepSeek-R1 popularized it, DAPO is its standard open recipe, and it is the evaluation workload of the async-RL systems literature (AReaL, StreamRL, PipelineRL), which consistently reports generation-dominated pipelines. On such a workload, can any staleness policy keep the trainer busy?

Setup: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on DAPO-Math-17k (in-tree math_dapo reward), single-turn, max_response_length=8192, same async harness and 1+1 H100 split as Phase 3, no tools. Gen-heaviness comes purely from CoT length: measured responses ~6.0K tokens mean, max pinned at the 8192 cap — generation ~200–220s per 64-sample batch vs ~124s training update. Learning was real (score −0.66 → −0.46). Zero crashes, zero redeploys, across all three runs.

Phase 4 conclusions

  • The trainer starves at every staleness value. The message queue never buffered a single sample in any run — a staleness budget only matters if the rollouter can get ahead, and a rollouter needing ~210s/batch against a ~124s consumer never gets ahead. s=8 and s=∞ are statistically identical.
  • Staleness does deliver its one win — overlapping generation with the training window (step 340s → 223s) — then saturates. The residual ~85–95s/step gap is the raw gen−train imbalance, landing on the trainer as one contiguous block per step.
  • These are the largest idle windows measured in the project (1.6–3.3 min blocks, 46–64% of trainer time), on the most realistic workload, with nothing tuned.

General findings

  • The imbalance law: the slower side pins its GPU; the faster side idles in per-step blocks of duration ≈ |gen − train|; staleness relocates idle onto the faster side but never below the throughput imbalance.
  • Workload class decides which side starves. Short-answer workloads (GSM8K, multi-hop QA) are train-heavy → rollout GPU idles. Long-CoT reasoning is gen-heavy → trainer GPU idles, at any staleness. Idle block size scales with response length / turn count / batch size — all legitimate workload parameters.
  • Staleness beyond 1 is pure loss in every measured case: step time saturates immediately (train-heavy: flat at s≥1; gen-heavy: flat at s≥8 ≈ s=∞), while data grows staler and queues deeper.
  • Weight sync is a non-issue — the idle is real. Measured NCCL param-sync means: 0.71–0.79s (Phase 3, all five configs) and 0.70–1.37s (Phase 4) against 72–340s steps — 0.2–1% of the cycle, versus idle blocks of 27–197s. In colocated mode (Phase 2) the analogous cost is the hybrid-engine switch (vLLM sleep/wake + weight reload, multi-second) — larger, but still not the source of the ~34% idle there.
  • External corroboration at frontier scale: SemiAnalysis's "RL Systems: Mind the Gap" (2026) reports every case study generation-bound on agentic SWE workloads at 100–256 GPU scale: trainer wait 30% (Qwen3-235B, 192 gen + 64 train H200s), 74% (GLM-5, 128+128), 30–60% (verl on GB300, 7,500s straggler tails), 60% (slime). Our Phase 4 measurements (46–64% trainer idle, straggler-bound batches, staleness unable to close the gap) are the same physics at 2-GPU scale.
  • Why async on-policy exists despite the bubble: disaggregation buys independent pool sizing, no hybrid-engine thrash, and streaming machinery — s=0 is an algorithm-fidelity choice (GRPO/DAPO degrade with stale data). The systems remedy, static pool right-sizing, is always stale in practice because the gen:train ratio drifts within a run (Phase 2: gen 77→190s over 16 steps; CoT lengths grow as models learn). Time-slicing is the dynamic complement: it harvests the residual bubble wherever it currently lives.

One-line summary

Realistic RL workloads — especially R1/DAPO-style long-CoT reasoning, the most-replicated open recipe family — leave one GPU pool idle 28–64% of the time in large, periodic, predictable blocks under every on-policy and bounded-staleness configuration of veRL, with nothing tuned. That is precisely the idle that GPU time-slicing converts into throughput.

8 · Appendix

Infrastructure & method

  • Cluster: verl-research-cluster-west (GKE, us-west1-c control plane), spot node pools h100-mega-8gpu-spot-b (a3-megagpu-8g, autoscaling 0–2 — used for Phases 2–4) and h100-2gpu-spot (Phase 1). Spot capacity typically provisioned in ~4 min; occasional multi-hour stockouts.
  • Measurement: 100ms nvidia-smi sampler (util/mem/power per GPU) alongside training logs; idle = <10% util (Phase 2: <15%); warmup steps excluded from steady-state stats.
  • Result durability: periodic kubectl cp rescues + on-completion gzip+base64 trace dump to stdout → preserved in Cloud Logging even if the spot node vanishes (adopted after two spot-preemption data losses).
  • Search backend (Phases 2–3): local pyserini BM25 over wikipedia-dpr-100w (~12GB prebuilt index) wrapped in a Serper-format HTTP server — hermetic, no external APIs.
  • verl versions: Phases 3–4 on verl main (commit 983cb0f2 for Phase 4) via verlai/verl images with runtime clone; Phase 2 on the CMU fork's pins (vllm 0.8.5, transformers 4.51.1) in a purpose-built image.
