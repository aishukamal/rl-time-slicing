1 · Per-sync-block step time, baseline vs both elastic arms

source: results/{s3,s4,s4b}/trajectory.tsv (col step_s); deltas computed vs baseline

Elastic time-slicing cuts every block's wall: totals v1-12 are baseline 8,420 s vs S4a 7,644 s (−9.22%) vs S4b 7,590 s (−9.87%); best single block −18.2% (S4a v10). S4a's controller was killed ~3 min early by an operator error, so its block 12 ran windowless (conservative bias).

2 · Cumulative wall-clock to 12 param syncs, and samples/GPU-hour

source: results/{s3,s4,s4b}/trajectory.tsv (cumulative step_s); samples/GPU-hr = 98,304 responses / 32 GPUs / wall

Same work (48 micro-steps x 2048 responses) finishes 831 s sooner on S4b: throughput 1,313 → 1,447 (+10.16%, S4a) → 1,457 samples/GPU-hr (+10.95%, S4b), reproducing ~+11% across two independent elastic arms.

3 · Trainer idle share per sync block: the narrowing harvest pool

source: results/{s3,s4,s4b}/trajectory.tsv (col tr_idle)

The baseline's harvestable idle narrows from 43.7% (v1) toward ~28% as DAPO's response length falls; the elastic arms run below it because windows convert part of that idle into serving (mean v1-11: baseline 32.3% → S4b 23.9%). Elastic lines show residual idle, not harvested idle.

4 · Switch walls across all recorded elastic windows

source: results/s4/s4_switch_timings.jsonl (27 windows), results/s4b/s4b_switch_timings.jsonl (26 windows)

Entering a window costs ~1.3-3.2 s (the ~2.4 s cluster = windows that also load a fresh weight version); leaving costs ~1-3 s typically, with a long tail up to 18.7 s from engine-side aborts of long in-flight sequences (tokens retained by partial rollout). Percentiles recomputed from raw logs; the report quotes S4b p50 1.46 / 2.98 s under its own percentile convention.

4b · Where the switch walls go: median phase breakdown (S4b)

source: results/s4b/s4b_switch_timings.jsonl (per-phase walls)

The stamp-only switch-in is dominated by trainer cache release + vLLM wake_up (~1.4 s); windows that land a new param version add ~2.4 s of wcache load. Switch-back medians are drain + sleep; its p90 tail (chart 4) comes from aborting long in-flight sequences.

5 · Reward trajectory overlay: the no-harm evidence

source: results/{s3,s4,s4b}/trajectory.tsv (col reward)

All three arms climb the same band (-0.15 → +0.40 baseline; -0.13 / -0.12 → +0.40 / +0.40 elastic): serving spare windows off truthfully-stamped weight versions does not perturb learning.

6 · Regime study: which workloads are elastic-eligible at a shipped 16:16 split

source: results/s1-30b/trajectory.tsv (S1b run), results/s1-7b/trajectory_and_waits.txt, results/s3/{trajectory.tsv,s3_train.log}; toll from s4b_switch_timings.jsonl

Eligibility needs both a real idle pool AND waits that dwarf the ~4.4 s switch round-trip: the 30B recipe's warmup regime is train-bound (idle 3-8%, ~890-token responses, micro-step waits not logged because there was no pool), stock Math-7B has idle but fragmented ~4 s waits, and long-CoT R1-Distill-7B has both (idle 32% mean, median wait ~40 s, ~6.6K-token responses): it passed the registered gate (idle ≥ 30%, median wait ≥ 30 s) and became the scored workload. Dots = per-version measurements behind each mean.

7 · Wait fragmentation: why Math-7B fails the gate and R1-Distill passes

source: results/s1-7b/trajectory_and_waits.txt (waits block, 40 micro-steps v1-10) and results/s3/s3_train.log (collection-wait lines, 48 micro-steps)

Math-7B's waits are fragmented: p50 4.1 s / p90 22.5 s, one modest pre-sync wait per block and ~4 s otherwise, so a ~4.4 s switch round-trip eats the gain. R1-Distill consolidates: p50 39.7 s / p90 129.7 s with a 100-235 s first-micro-step wait every block, leaving room to serve inside the window.

8 · Exposed trainer gen-wait per block: what the windows actually converted

source: results/{s3,s4,s4b}/trajectory.tsv (col wait_s)

Over the window-active range v2-11 the exposed wait pool shrinks from 2,270 s (baseline) to 1,521 s (S4a, −33%) and 1,409 s (S4b, −38%); the remainder is pre-fire collection (windows open at median ~52/128 samples collected) plus finite spare capacity. The report's 2348 → 1478 s figure uses on-node accounting; see data notes.

9 · Window close reasons: what the S4a→S4b controller tuning changed

source: results/{s4,s4b}/*_decisions.jsonl (action=switch_to_trainer, field reason)

S4a closed almost every window on the hard-collect failsafe (fill-EMA underestimating the spare-boosted fill); S4b's registered tuning (VD-6 hold/release guard, hard-collect 124, post-reset fill warmup 5) flipped the mix to mostly predictive-ETA closes, including one batch-completed-late close handled by a 7.1 s stall-not-crash hold.

Data notes (read before quoting)

  • S4a operator error: a wrong pgrep pattern killed the S4a controller ~3 minutes early, so its sync block 12 ran windowless (conservative bias for S4a in charts 1, 2, 8). S4a artifacts nonetheless record 27 switch-in cycles / 27 window-invariant checks vs S4b's 26/26; the report's text counts 26 verified S4a cycles.
  • Percentile convention: p50 here is the interpolated median and p90 is nearest-rank, recomputed from the raw switch-timing logs; the report quotes S4b switch p50 1.46 s (in) / 2.98 s (back) and p90 2.50 / 11.03 s under its own convention. Raw dots are plotted, so nothing hides in the convention.
  • Wait-conversion accounting: the report's “2348 → 1478 s (37% converted)” comes from on-node accounting. Local artifacts give v2-11 sums of 2270 → 1409 s from trajectory.tsv wait_s and 2258 → 1386 s from the train.log collection waits: same story, slightly different measure boundaries.
  • results/s1-30b/trajectory.tsv contains TWO probe runs (S1: v1-2, including a 73.8 s v2 that is an MQ-drain artifact; S1b: v1-3). Chart 6 uses the S1b run. The S1 run's values (idle 7.2%/1.8%, len 837/1526) are consistent with the train-bound verdict.
  • The waits block in results/s1-7b/trajectory_and_waits.txt is unlabeled in the file; it parses as 40 per-micro-step waits (10 blocks × 4) with p50 4.08 s / p90 22.5 s, matching VD-STATE.md's logged “p50 micro-step wait 4s, p90 24s”, which fixes the interpretation. The trajectory block covers v1-9 (killed at v9), so the 10th wait group is the in-flight v10 block.
  • v12 in every arm is the standard termination flush (a partial block, ~150-160 s); it is plotted but starred wherever it appears.

Not plotted (wanted, but no local measured data)

  • wcache dump + prefetch wall per param sync: the scored-arm local artifacts do not carry a per-sync series (the report quotes the 18.4-23.1 s range; s2 shakedown logs exist but are out of scope for scored charts).
  • 30B micro-step wait distribution (chart 6, middle panel): not logged in results/s1-30b/; only per-block wait_s exists (0.9-40.5 s), consistent with “no harvest pool”.
